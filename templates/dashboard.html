Cybersecurity Framework

Security controls designed to protect digital heritage from illicit tampering, loss of provenance, and bit rot.

SHA-256 Cryptographic Integrity

Planned: Phase 2

Every newly deposited record will be digested through the SHA-256 cryptographic hashing algorithm upon intake. The resulting checksum establishes an unforgeable baseline to verify that files have not suffered accidental silent corruption (bit rot) or unauthorized malicious modification.

Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)

Planned: Phase 2

Strict compartmentalization of user privileges to enforce the Principle of Least Privilege. System roles (Public Researcher, Field Archaeologist, and System Administrator) dictate who may inspect records, deposit novel archaeological data, or manage system parameters.

Sanitized Secure Uploads

Planned: Phase 2

Defensive intake controls protecting server infrastructure against arbitrary file uploads, path traversal vulnerabilities, and embedded polyglot payloads. Includes filename canonicalization, strict MIME-type validation, and size enforcement routines.

Immutable Activity Auditing

Planned: Phase 2

Time-stamped audit logs documenting all archive interactions, including catalog searches, verification routines, failed login sequences, and administrative configuration updates to guarantee institutional accountability.

Academic Context

Why Apply Cybersecurity to Cultural Heritage?

Archaeological documentation represents irreplaceable evidence of human history. When physical artifacts undergo deterioration or destruction, secondary digital records—such as 3D photogrammetric models, excavation journals, and uncompressed field imagery—become the primary historical source.

Standard commercial cloud storage solutions often overlook the specialized threats encountered by archaeological repositories: silent bit drift, deliberate falsification of provenance records, unauthorized asset duplication, and illicit antiquities trafficking intelligence leaks.

The CIA Triad in Digital Archaeology

  • Confidentiality: Safeguarding unexcavated geospatial coordinates against looting and unauthorized commercial exploitation.
  • Integrity: Using cryptographic hashing (SHA-256) to ensure historical documents and artifact logs remain strictly identical to their recorded state.
  • Availability: Ensuring scholarly researchers maintain resilient, uninterrupted access to vital heritage data across generational timeframes.
